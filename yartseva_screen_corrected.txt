CRITICAL CORRECTION TO PRIOR SCREEN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

My prior Yartseva screen was wrong on several criteria. I had used the
Phelps/Mayer/Lynch heuristics ("high ROE compounders," "earnings
growth at any cost," "asset-light SaaS roll-up") as Yartseva criteria.

The actual paper's EMPIRICAL findings DISPROVE several of those:

  ✗ EARNINGS GROWTH (EPS, revenue, gross/operating/net profit, FCF
    growth, 5-yr CAGR — none) is NOT statistically significant in
    Yartseva's dynamic panel models. Quote: "the suggestion from the
    popular literature that to deliver high share price growth, the
    company must demonstrate significant growth of earnings for
    extended period, is not supported by the empirical evidence."

  ✗ DEBT-TO-EQUITY, debt cover, Altman score, capital allocation
    decisions (buybacks, dividends), R&D propensity, marketing
    propensity — ALL not significant.

  ✗ P/E RATIO — not useful as predictor (negative earnings make it
    unreliable; extreme values skew regressions).

  ✗ ROE, ROC, gross/net/operating profit margin, cash ROIC — none
    significant in dynamic models.

YARTSEVA'S ACTUAL CRITERIA (statistically significant predictors)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

FUNDAMENTAL FACTORS:

1. SMALL SIZE (Y1) — negative coefficient on log(TEV)
   - Average small-cap multibagger had market cap < $250M
   - Medium-cap winners ~$2B; Large-cap ~$32B
   - Strongest effect at the small end

2. HIGH BOOK-TO-MARKET (Y2a) — positive coefficient
   - B/M ≥ 0.40 minimum threshold for high-probability multibagger
   - Average winner B/M = 1.10 (book value exceeds market cap = undervalued)
   - Low B/M (≤ 0.06 — typical of growth/glamour stocks) = WORST
     performance, often negative
   - Each +1% in B/M associated with +7-52% next-year return

3. HIGH FREE CASH FLOW YIELD (Y2b) — positive coefficient — MOST IMPORTANT
   - FCF/Price ratio is the single most important driver in Yartseva's
     model
   - Quote: "Robust cash flow yield is the most important driver of
     multibagger stock outperformance"

4. ROBUST PROFITABILITY (Y3) — positive coefficient
   - In dynamic models: ROA (small coefficient 0.4-1.9)
   - In static models: EBITDA margin
   - Avoid loss-makers (operating profitability < 0 = worst portfolios)

5. AGGRESSIVE INVESTMENT (Y4a) — positive coefficient — REVERSE OF FF5
   - Year-on-year asset growth POSITIVE for multibagger returns
   - Contradicts traditional Fama-French (which says conservative inv
     wins). For multibaggers, aggressive growth is good.

6. AFFORDABILITY OF INVESTMENT (Y4b) — investment dummy
   - Inv dummy = 1 IF asset growth EXCEEDS EBITDA growth
   - When Inv dummy = 1: next-year returns LOWER by 4-11 percentage pts
   - Quote: "firms must invest and grow their assets; however, the
     investment must remain affordable and covered by growing EBITDA"

TECHNICAL FACTORS:

7. ENTRY POINT NEAR 12-MONTH LOW (Y5)
   - PRICE RANGE = (current price − 12mo low) / (12mo high − 12mo low) × 100%
   - HIGH price range (near 12mo high) → LOW next-year return
   - Closer to 12mo low = better entry
   - Quote: "the entry point is critical for future returns. Specifically,
     the stock should be close to its 12-month low at the time of purchase
     and, ideally, have fallen in price considerably in the preceding six
     months"

8. SHORT-TERM TREND REVERSAL (Y6)
   - 1-month momentum: weakly positive (only 1 model significant)
   - 3-month and 6-month momentum: NEGATIVE → stocks that fell in past
     3-6 months tend to outperform next year (contrarian)

MACRO FACTORS:

9. STABLE OR DECLINING INTEREST RATES (Y7)
   - Rising Fed rate dummy = REDUCES next-year returns by 8-12 pp
   - Multibaggers do best in stable/declining rate environments


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE CORRECTED FRAMEWORK SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Yartseva's multibagger is essentially:

  SMALL-CAP DEEP-VALUE STOCK
  + GENERATING HIGH FREE CASH FLOW relative to price
  + PROFITABLE (positive operating profit / ROA)
  + INVESTING IN GROWTH but EBITDA covers the investment
  + BOUGHT NEAR ITS 12-MONTH LOW
  + AFTER A 3-6 MONTH PRICE DECLINE
  + IN A STABLE/DECLINING RATE ENVIRONMENT

This is profoundly DIFFERENT from "Phelps high-ROE asset-light SaaS
compounder" framework. Yartseva is much more value/contrarian.

WHAT THIS REVEALS (vs my prior brief)
================================================================================

1. THE SaaS COMPOUNDER FRAMEWORK IS WRONG FOR YARTSEVA
   VHI.V, ZDC.V, JYNT — high-quality SaaS roll-ups — DO NOT fit
   Yartseva. They have low B/M (high growth multiples), modest FCF
   yield (high P/FCF), and are bought at trends not bottoms. These
   are Mayer/Phelps multibaggers, not Yartseva.

2. THE BIOTECH PLATFORM FRAMEWORK IS WRONG FOR YARTSEVA
   ARTV, RAPT, NKTR, OM, CELC — pre-revenue platform bets — FAIL
   Yartseva (no FCF, no B/M >1, no operating profit). Different
   multibagger pathway (binary catalyst).

3. THE GROWTH MEGA-CAP FRAMEWORK IS WRONG FOR YARTSEVA
   CVNA, HHH, HTHT, DRVN — all too large for Yartseva sweet spot.
   Even HHH (which scores 5/7) gets a FAIL on size; the rest score
   3/7 or below.

4. YARTSEVA'S MULTIBAGGERS ARE DEEP VALUE WITH FCF
   The winners look like: NEXN, LOGC, SRL, NRP, TBPH, BRN, ALCO.
   Small-cap value with cash flow, near 12-month lows, undervalued
   by book or NAV.

5. ROYALTY/CASH-FLOW STRUCTURES ARE OVERREPRESENTED
   3 of top 5 (SRL, NRP, TBPH) are royalty/cash-flow streams. Yartseva's
   FCF-yield criterion strongly favors this structure.

6. ENTRY POINT MATTERS — most of the workbook's high-conviction smart-
   money positions are NOT at 12-month lows. They've already rallied.
   HHH ($63 vs 52w $61-91) is one exception.
