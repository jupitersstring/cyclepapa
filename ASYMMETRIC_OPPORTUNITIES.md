# Asymmetric Opportunities — PSU × Conditionalities × Insider Behaviour

*Generated 2026-06-02. Inputs: psu_step_change.csv (fresh PSU adopters),
psu_conditionalities.json (business-specific vesting triggers),
form4_buys.json (Form 4 P-purchases), psu_forensics_v2.json (LTI mix,
SOP, plan changes). Boilerplate matches (e.g. "separation from service",
generic "calendar year" RSU language) filtered out for the rankings
below.*

The thesis is that the highest-conviction asymmetric setups require all
three signals to point the same direction in the same window:

  1. PSU structure is fresh (filing ≤ 180d), clean, and aligned to a
     specific business event the market hasn't repriced.
  2. The vesting condition is business-SPECIFIC (named transaction,
     dollar EBITDA / FCF target, debt-paydown threshold, regulatory
     milestone, named asset sale) — not boilerplate TSR vs S&P.
  3. Insiders are voting with their wallets (multiple distinct buyers
     in the same recent window, ideally C-suite + board).

## Tier 1 — All three signals aligned

### NCLH — Norwegian Cruise Line ($8.6B mcap, $18.81)
**Why it's asymmetric**: three independent directors bought ~$970K total
in May 2026 — MacDonald $248K, Byng-Thorne $521K, Lansberry $197K — a
genuine board cluster. PSU is 60% of LTI on the cleanest metric stack in
our universe (TSR + EPS + ROIC + EBITDA — four per-share/return metrics
together, no single-trigger CIC). Cruise capex cycle is past the worst
and the comp committee tied the LTI to ROIC, which means the board has
told management deleveraging matters.
**Key risk**: regulatory_milestone matches on this name were boilerplate
RSU vesting language — the asymmetry comes from PSU quality + insider
cluster, not from a specific event trigger.

### HFFG — HF Foods Group ($102M mcap, $1.90)
**Why it's asymmetric**: CEO Lin Xi, CFO McGarry, CAO Chang, and Director
Lam all bought on 2026-03-18/19 — four distinct executives in a 48-hour
window, the cleanest small-cap cluster in the data. The PSU has an
**explicit revenue target ladder**: $1.232B for 2025 (2.5% growth, 100%
payout), $1.281B for 2026 (4% growth). Half of LTI is PSU with TSR +
Revenue + EBITDA. Mcap = $102M, so even tiny option-like buys signal
conviction on a per-NEO basis.
**Key risk**: $4-15K buys per insider are small in absolute terms;
need fundamentals on Chinese-American foodservice distribution to
underwrite the $1.232B hurdle. SOP support 87%.

### CRM — Salesforce ($148B mcap, $181.82)
**Why it's asymmetric**: Directors Alber and Kirk each bought ~$500K on
2026-03-18/19 — $1M cluster at one of the largest mid/large-caps in our
set. Step-change stack is the largest in absolute dollars: $25B buyback
(17% of mcap) authorised 47d ago, spin-off declared 47d ago, governance
reset 47d ago. PSU is 67% of LTI on TSR + EPS + Revenue with **explicit
business targets**: $46B fiscal '27 revenue guide, 34.1% non-GAAP /
20.1% GAAP operating margin (+110bps yoy), $72.4B RPO.
**Key risk**: Say-on-pay support only 77% — shareholder unease about
comp. $1M insider buy / $149B mcap is 0.001% — directors are nibbling,
not loading up. Watch for follow-through buying.

## Tier 2 — Business-specific PSU triggers, insider signal weaker

### CCI — Crown Castle ($39.5B mcap, $90.57)
The cleanest break-up-in-motion in the set. PSU language ties vesting
to "sale of $2.5 billion of non-strategic assets and the pay down of
$1 billion in debt" — an explicit, dollarised capital-allocation
conditionality. Special committee disclosed 57d ago, active-bid
language 32d ago, governance reset 57d ago, spin-off declared 57d ago,
one director (Stephens) bought $74K. The board has codified the
break-up in the LTI plan.
**Key risk**: only one insider buy, and at $39.5B mcap the asymmetry
needs the spin/sale to actually clear at premium values vs. the existing
tower comp set.

### ARRY — Array Technologies ($1.3B mcap, $8.57)
PSU has an **explicit $178M adjusted EBITDA target** for 2025 (also
disclosed $188M target including APA). Step-change stack includes
spin-off declared 56d, **go-private language 56d**, governance reset
56d, strategic-alts language 56d. Solar deep-discount sector special
situation with a public take-private flag and a hard dollar EBITDA
hurdle.
**Key risk**: zero insider buying. If insiders had been buying, this
would be Tier 1.

### WTRG — Essential Utilities ($10.6B mcap, $37.47)
Pure M&A arb: **pending merger with American Water**, and the proxy
explicitly says "upon closing of the transaction, Mr. Franklin will
become Executive Vice Chair of the combined company". PSU vesting is
event-locked to merger close. Three per-share metrics, double-trigger
CIC, transformation signal.
**Key risk**: arb spread is the trade — break risk if the FTC blocks.
No insider buying because insiders are locked.

### DNUT — Krispy Kreme ($658M mcap, $3.82)
**$140.3M adjusted EBITDA target** disclosed in PSU. Spin/separation
language present. Distressed turnaround at sub-$1B mcap with a
specific EBITDA hurdle that is publicly committed-to. Per-share metric
+ EBITDA + ROIC stack, double-trigger CIC.
**Key risk**: McDonald's relationship overhang and franchisee unit
economics — the $140M EBITDA target is achievable on paper but
operationally aggressive.

### SPOK — Spok Holdings ($221M mcap, $10.65)
CEO Vince Kelly bought $107K (May 2026). PSU 50% of LTI with TSR + EPS
+ Revenue + EBITDA — full per-share stack on a $221M micro-cap. PSU
targets explicitly named at **$72.5M wireless revenue + $29.0M
adjusted EBITDA**, both already exceeded in the most recent year.
**Key risk**: paging-services declining secular business; the
"transformation" is unit-economics defence, not growth.

## What's NOT a setup
- "Regulatory milestone" matches on **CRM, NCLH, SPOK, ENPH** were
  largely boilerplate RSU vesting language about calendar-year prorating
  on death/disability/retirement — not real FDA/Phase-3 triggers. The
  pure biotech regulatory-milestone PSU plans are rarer in our set
  (BIIB partial, GOSS) and the screen flagged them anyway via other
  signals.
- "Spin_separation" matches on names like KMPR / IRWD / INSW were
  mostly "separation from service" boilerplate — not actual spin-offs.

working tree is restorable from origin:

    every fetch

    upstream data, unchanged

`.DS_Store`, and `*.log` — everything else (cache + JSONs + CSVs) is
permanent.
