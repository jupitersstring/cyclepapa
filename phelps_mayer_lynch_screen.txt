================================================================================
THE PHELPS / MAYER / LYNCH / OSWAL FRAMEWORK COMBINED
================================================================================

PHELPS (1972 "100 to 1 in the Stock Market"):
  P1. Small and relatively unknown companies
  P2. Offer new products / new materials / new production methods
  P3. Things that help solve problems and improve humans' lives
  P4. Strong earnings growth
  P5. Potential for further expansion
  P6. Sound management practices
  P7. Hold for extended periods, avoid overtrading
  Quote: "vision to see them, courage to buy them, patience to hold them"

MAYER (2018 "100 Baggers"):
  M1. "Coffee-can portfolio" — hold for at least 10 years
  M2. Extended periods of earnings growth WITH valuation multiple
      expansion (the "twin engines" of share price growth)
  M3. Accelerating rather than steady growth in earnings
  M4. High ROE (>20%) sustained
  M5. Owner-operators: visionary CEO + HIGH INSIDER OWNERSHIP
  M6. Beaten-down and forgotten stocks AFTER they turn around and
      return to profitability
  M7. Small-cap rather than mega-cap

LYNCH (1988, 1993 — managed Magellan 28% CAGR):
  L1. "Growth at reasonable prices" (GARP)
  L2. Simple business with boring name, doing something off-putting
  L3. Find them in everyday life — buy what you know
  L4. Six categories: slow growers, stalwarts, fast growers, cyclicals,
      turnarounds, asset plays
  L5. Earnings consistency
  L6. Low PEG ratio
  L7. Insiders buying
  L8. Low institutional ownership / analyst coverage

OSWAL (2014 "Wealth Creation," focused on Indian markets):
  O1. QGLP: Quality + Growth + Longevity + reasonable Price
  O2. Small, relatively unknown companies
  O3. Sustainable high earnings growth
  O4. Quality management
  O5. Trading at low single-digit P/E
  O6. 100x phenomenon REQUIRES BOTH earnings growth AND multiple
      expansion

COMBINED PHELPS/MAYER/LYNCH/OSWAL CRITERIA:
  Q1. SMALL CAP (P1, M7, O2)
  Q2. HIGH ROE > 20% sustained (M4)
  Q3. STRONG EARNINGS GROWTH + ACCELERATING (P4, M3, O3, O6)
  Q4. OWNER-OPERATOR / HIGH INSIDER OWNERSHIP (M5, L7)
  Q5. VISIONARY MANAGEMENT (P6, M5, O4)
  Q6. BORING/UNCOOL/UNDER-FOLLOWED (L2, L8, P1)
  Q7. REASONABLE VALUATION / LOW PEG (L1, L6, O5)
  Q8. INNOVATIVE PRODUCTS / SOLVES PROBLEMS (P2, P3)
  Q9. SUSTAINABLE COMPETITIVE MOAT
  Q10. LONG REINVESTMENT RUNWAY (P5, O1 longevity)
  Q11. "TWIN ENGINES": both earnings growth AND multiple expansion (M2, O6)
  Q12. ASSET-LIGHT / CAPITAL EFFICIENT
  Q13. BEATEN-DOWN TURNAROUND (M6, L4)
  Q14. ASSET PLAY (Lynch's 6th category)

================================================================================
KEY TAKEAWAY
================================================================================

The combination of frameworks reveals different multibagger pathways:

  YARTSEVA framework identifies cheap cash-generating value stocks
  (3-5x compounding via mean reversion + FCF accumulation)

  PHELPS/MAYER/LYNCH frameworks identify high-quality compounders +
  beaten-down turnarounds + asset plays
  (10-100x compounding via earnings growth + multiple expansion + time)

Both produce multibaggers via different mechanisms:
  - Yartseva: market mispricing of cash-flow-positive value stocks
  - Phelps/Mayer/Lynch: extended compounding of high-quality businesses

NEXN and LOGC are the only names in the workbook that screen well on
BOTH frameworks — they offer both the Yartseva margin of safety AND
