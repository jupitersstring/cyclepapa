# Why these are most asymmetric for the common

The scoring layers proxy for something specific: *information asymmetry
between insiders/board and the marginal public buyer*. A score doesn't
make a name asymmetric -- the underlying setup does. For the common
equity, asymmetric means a hard floor (book / tender price / verified
shrinkage) paired with an outsized catalyst the market hasn't priced.

Below: the structural case for each top-of-rank name, signal-by-signal.

---

## 1. GPUS (Hyperscale Data) -- composite 64

**$91M mcap | $0.17 spot | P/B 0.50 | 1% above 52w low**

- **The company is offering you 22% above market for your stock right
  now**. Live fixed-price self-tender at $0.21 vs the $0.17 print,
  amended 2 days ago. That's not a hope; it's a published bid.
- **10b5-1 termination +20**: a director walked back scheduled
  selling at the same time the company is bidding for shares. Insiders
  are not feeding the tender.
- **P/B 0.50** at the 52w low: the equity sits at half book on a
  capital-light data-center name.

Asymmetric structure: *downside* is the tender floor (+22% if you
hand them shares); *upside* is the residual stub if the buyback retires
the offer and the rump re-rates. The risk is dilution if the company
raises again -- but the director termination is the tell that
management thinks the next move is up, not down.

## 2. GETY (Getty Images) -- composite 63

**$299M mcap | $0.71 spot | P/B 0.55 | 2% above 52w low**

- **10b5-1 leg +74** (capped to +25 in the composite). Multiple senior
  vice presidents *terminated sell plans*: Mikael Cho, Michael Teaster
  (169,000 shares each) -- not one cancellation but a pattern of board
  insiders cancelling scheduled sales.
- **Self-tender live**: company buying its own equity at this price.
- Down 78% from 52w high; book at 0.55x.

Asymmetric structure: this is the Bastian "distressed equity stub"
archetype. Private-equity-controlled (post-SPAC), debt-laden, board
buying. The downside is capped by liquidation value of the IP library
(the largest curated stock-photo asset in the world). The upside is
either deleveraging brings the equity back, or a strategic buyer
emerges. The board's *simultaneous* sell-plan cancellations are the
information signal: they don't terminate plans 73 days into the buyback
window unless they believe the stock re-rates above their grant
prices.

## The pattern across the list

Every name on this list combines **three of**:

1. A hard floor: P/B at or below 1.0, near 52w low, or a tender bid.
2. A verified action signal: real share-count shrinkage, a 10b5-1
   *termination* (not boilerplate), a board cluster buy, an active
   tender or 13E-3.
3. A forward catalyst that closes the discount: a named PSU hurdle,
   a live take-private process, an activist on the cap table, or
   a buyback large enough to consume the float discount.

The thesis isn't that any single signal is predictive on its own --
the backtest already showed only some buckets carry alpha. The thesis
is that the *intersection* of structural commitment (PSU/governance) +
mechanical execution (verified buyback / tender) + insider behaviour
(cluster buys or sell-plan terminations) is rare and informational.
Names where all three align are the asymmetric ones.
